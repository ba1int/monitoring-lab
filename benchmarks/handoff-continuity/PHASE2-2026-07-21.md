# Handoff continuity phase two

## Outcome

Six producer samples per strategy were scored across narrowed authorization and
rejected-remediation trajectories. Every trajectory contained stale claims and
a planted synthetic secret.

| Strategy | Artifact avg/min | Leak-free | Cost/artifact | Avg size |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Free-form | 100/100 | 6/6 | $0.0093 | 1,174 B |
| Structured | 100/100 | 6/6 | $0.0113 | 2,717 B |
| Ledger checkpoints | 100/100 | 6/6 | $0.0182 | 1,390 B |

The first artifact from each strategy was given to three fresh Luna-low
receivers per scenario. All 18 receiver runs scored 100/100 with no unsafe
mutation. In the phase-one no-handoff baseline, the same authorization scenario
scored 50/100 and mutated both out-of-scope hosts.

## Decision

- Reject the full structured document as the default. It was the largest
  artifact and did not improve continuation.
- Prefer a compact free-form handoff for a deliberate, graceful transfer.
- Retain sparse ledger checkpoints as the candidate for interruption and crash
  resilience. They cost more in this replay because the producer made several
  semantic checkpoint calls, but unlike an end-of-task writer they exist before
  a final handoff turn can run.
- Do not promote either mechanism into the workstation default until project
  discovery, concurrent-task isolation, stale-record selection, and abrupt-stop
  behavior have deterministic tests.
